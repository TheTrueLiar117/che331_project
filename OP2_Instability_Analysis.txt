STABILITY, UNIQUENESS, AND MULTIPLICITY ANALYSIS
================================================

1. Physical and Mathematical Proof of OP 2 Instability
------------------------------------------------------

In a non-isothermal Continuous Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR), steady-state operation
requires the simultaneous satisfaction of the nonlinear species mass balance and
the linear energy balance:

  Mass Balance (Heat Generation):
      X_CO2 = [ tau / (eps * c_CO2,in) ] * R_meth(T, p, w_alpha)

  Energy Balance (Heat Removal):
      X_CO2 = [ (1 + St) / dT_ad ] * (T - T_op)

Under conditions of thermo-kinetic multiplicity, these two curves intersect at
three operating points:
  - OP 1: Lower extinguished state (stable)
  - OP 2: Intermediate state (unstable)
  - OP 3: Upper ignited state (stable)

While OP 1 and OP 3 are asymptotically stable, OP 2 is inherently unstable.
This instability can be established through both physical perturbation reasoning
(following van Heerden, 1953) and mathematical sensitivity analysis:


A. Physical Perturbation Mechanism (van Heerden, 1953)
------------------------------------------------------
Because reaction heat release is directly proportional to conversion
(Q_dot_gen proportional to X_CO2 * dT_ad), the S-shaped mass balance curve
represents dimensionless chemical heat generation, while the straight energy
balance line represents dimensionless heat removal by convection and jacket
cooling. At OP 2, the mass balance curve crosses the energy balance line from
below to above as temperature increases.

  - Positive Temperature Disturbance (+delta T at OP 2):
    If a small positive temperature disturbance (+delta T) occurs at OP 2, the
    mass balance curve lies above the energy balance line. Chemical heat
    generation exceeds heat removal (Q_gen > Q_rem), causing net thermal
    accumulation (dT/dt > 0) that drives a self-accelerating temperature
    excursion until the reactor settles at the upper stable ignited state (OP 3).

  - Negative Temperature Disturbance (-delta T at OP 2):
    Conversely, if a small negative temperature disturbance (-delta T) occurs at
    OP 2, the mass balance curve falls below the energy balance line. Heat
    removal exceeds heat generation (Q_rem > Q_gen), giving net cooling
    (dT/dt < 0), cooling the reactor down until it extinguishes at the lower
    stable state (OP 1).


B. Mathematical Slope Condition
-------------------------------
Geometrically, the mass balance curve crosses the energy balance line from below
to above at OP 2 only because the mass-based thermal sensitivity (slope of the
mass balance curve, dX/dT) is steeper than the energy-based thermal sensitivity
(slope of the energy balance line, (1 + St) / dT_ad).

Taking the total differential of the implicit mass balance at constant
residence time tau and inlet concentration c_in yields the exact rate-based
thermal sensitivity:

      dX     [ tau / (eps * c_in) ] * (dR / dT)_X
      --  =  -------------------------------------
      dT     1 - [ tau / (eps * c_in) ] * (dR / dX)_T

Here (dR / dT)_X and (dR / dX)_T denote the partial derivatives of R_meth with
respect to T and X, respectively.

Local Stability Criterion:
  A steady state is locally stable if and only if:
      (dX / dT)|_mass < (1 + St) / dT_ad

Evaluation at the Three Operating Points:
  - At OP 2: Rapid Arrhenius rate acceleration (dR/dT >> 0) dominates over
    reactant depletion (dR/dX < 0), causing:
        (dX / dT)|_OP2 > (1 + St) / dT_ad
    which violates the local stability criterion and makes OP 2 unstable.

  - At OP 1: Low temperature keeps the Arrhenius reaction rate small, so dX/dT
    remains strictly below (1 + St) / dT_ad, ensuring asymptotic stability.

  - At OP 3: Near thermodynamic equilibrium and reactant depletion, where
    dR/dT <= 0, the mass-balance slope remains strictly below the cooling
    threshold (1 + St) / dT_ad, ensuring asymptotic stability.
